# Physical continuity in an animated performance

For a scene set in the ordinary physical world, identify who supports each load and what is attached, on the ground, in flight or at rest. Draw the actors and props together in the same coordinate system before adding texture and moving the camera.

- Attach the head, neck, collar and shoulders to the same torso transform. Allow the jaw to overlap the neck and the collar to cover its base, including in profile and mirrored poses. Flex the elbow and knee with consistent upper and lower segment lengths. If a target is beyond reach, move the performer or the object into reach, or stage a step.
- Place each planted sole on the painted walkable surface. For a moving vehicle, anchor the footprint to its moving floor. Separate the foreground path, curb, water and distant actors with perspective, occlusion, shadows and consistent scale. A crouch needs a visible pelvis, bent knees and supporting feet or a planted knee. Ground and support should remain readable after a camera crop.
- After impact, check whether gravity can leave the object in the drawn orientation. If its weight sits outside a single support point, show the tip, skid, sag or second contact that brings it to rest. On relaunch, peel the surface off its support from the same resting pose.
- Give each prop actual contact points. Fingers wrap around a handle or pinch the material on opposite sides. Leave negative space between fingers and string. A knot passes through an attachment and tightens. A liquid or flame stays inside its container. A carried load has weight and a support underneath it. At a handoff use the actual reached grip. Maintain contact until the next hand or surface supports the object. Inspect the frame just before ownership changes, the transfer frame and the frame after.
- Check the rendered silhouette and the order in which objects cover one another. A correct joint behind an opaque post, a bench through a sleeve or a line painted the same colour and thickness as a finger can defeat a mathematically valid pose. Move the angle or neutral background, change the layering or redraw the interaction until it reads without dialogue.

Review the actual encoded frames at delivery resolution, including the reach extremes, both sides of cuts and the full contact sequence at native speed. Cover every repeated use of a corrected character or prop. Measure limb lengths, grip separation, support clearance and foot drift where the animation exposes those coordinates. Bind evidence to the delivered encode and document visual sampling separately. A global movement score measures scene activity and cannot establish anatomical or physical continuity.
