# Impasto oil painting

Design the story around a physical action that leaves visible pigment behind. Introduce the action at hand scale, establish where it takes place, then move close enough to see its cost to the character. Find an overhead or reverse angle where repeated actions add up to a second image. Return to a familiar view after something physical has changed. Vary shot lengths on the shared sound clock. A three-minute film needs distinct actions and purposeful reframing across that length.

Build a flat illustration for each space and each moving part. Compose the large dark, middle and light masses first. Close faces need a socket, lid, nose plane, cheek, lip, jaw and neck that share the head pivot. Use elbows, wrists, feet and tool contact as animation anchors. Paint distant figures economically. The near edge of a garment can cut across the camera. Keep geometry continuous beneath overlaps.

Lay every surface with the bundled knife engine. Paint the whole set, actors, lettering and shadows. Coarse square-ended blades establish the broad planes. Smaller blades pick out the borders and features. Give walls a vertical drag, floors shallow horizontal drags, curved objects a tangential field. At 1280×720 start background radius passes near [30,15,7,3.5,1.8], and use tight passes on eyes and narrow props. Seed each plate once. Keep marks fixed on their subject through a camera move. Split nearby forms into separate groups so large marks stop at the silhouette. Use a thin underpainting behind the ridges. Put the relief light high at camera left. Try raising wet specular on a damp or polished object only where the story motivates it.

Reserve an accent for a change the audience can point to. A cool near-monochrome world can carry one pure pigment, then individual objects can take colour on contact. Bind the colour delay to position so a mark travels down the length of an object. Paint deposits and tracks can accumulate through a shot. Hold long enough to read the change. Score the act of laying a mark, the anticipation, impact, and the moment after. Leave gaps for English voice when the brief calls for narration. Sidecar captions keep the painting clear. Large, gently irregular painted serif lettering works for the title. Keep end credits short.

Review actual close crops for blade ridges, tiny holes, displaced heads and clipped silhouettes. Watch the transition into and out of a moving action. For transfer tests, give the new author only this skill's working copy and technical tools. The author chooses the topic independently and renders before seeing comparative feedback. Record what was supplied. Keep production inputs and external sources in separate folders.

In a close prop or face shot, check the outer silhouette under strong raking light. Keep ridges fine on skin and give the prop enough screen area to read without the voice. In the wide or overhead return, check the projected size and saturation of the accumulated marks against their earlier close-up. Keep an edge margin throughout the camera travel. A slow clip can still need short, precisely timed impacts.

Test the colour payoff at two scales. At thumbnail size the object that received the pigment should still read first. At full size its lit and shaded planes should retain distinct knife marks. In a returning wide shot, tint large background planes separately from the active subject. Check a frame before the change, a frame mid-spread and a frame after it settles. Compare the duration of the spread and the camera movement as well as the final palette.

For a turning subject, paint the deposited marks first and the moving silhouette second. Vary the length of the marks and leave visible gaps that follow the turn. Reopen the encoded shot at full speed. Check whether the marks explain the path, the subject stays legible at the fastest point, and the motion resolves before the cut.

## Movement and the soundtrack

Start a camera move before the cut when the action is already underway. Set up physical cause and response across the cut. Animate load, contact, recoil and settling on separate beats. Give a held pose a story reason and keep pigment attached to the surface. At native playback speed, inspect the longest quiet runs in the new cut and the reference. Use consecutive-frame optical flow together with the fraction of moving pixels to locate holds. Exclude intentional end cards and record the threshold and image size. Judge the cuts and the performance in motion before changing speed.

Write the score around an emotional turn in the story. Establish where the music enters, where the physical action needs silence, and where the motif changes hands. For a generated instrumental, audition the actual exported recording, save the accepted prompt and original file, and keep source and film in/out points for each edit. Set the music against the original voice takes. Shape its level before speech and allow phrase endings to breathe. Spot separate effects from the visible contact and the listening position. Keep original field recording credits, licences, edits, voice, score, effects and master with the production.

Give the film's showcase a visible music-making section. Name the generator actually used, identify separately written or sampled parts, give the excerpt times and let the audience audition the accepted cue, edited music and effects. When sharing a revision, keep original-speed playback of both versions at the same timecode. Identify playback tests, numerical loudness checks and listening judgments separately in review notes.

## Physical contact in a moving painting

Review each actor’s support, head-to-collar overlap and arm reach in the rig and again in the encoded composition. Use the guide in PHYSICAL-CONTINUITY.md in a working copy. For planar animation, src/kinematics.js provides composed transforms and a fixed-length two-link reach with joint limits. Attach props to the hand it actually reaches. Animate planting, an actual grip or material sliding through fingers, release, and the next support as separate events. Keep fingers, string, handles, heat sources and adjacent sleeves visually distinguishable through the motion. A cropped hand or sleeve needs a plausible continuation beyond the frame.

For a contact-heavy production, scripts/check_contacts.mjs can evaluate the film's own pose adapter at its delivery frame rate. The adapter exports createReview() returning {frames, fps, sample(frame, seconds)}. Return an array of {id, unit, value, min, max} observations with at least one bound. Use an empty array for shots where that relationship is absent. Return distinct names for joints, props and support surfaces that the production actually exposes. Invoke node scripts/check_contacts.mjs ADAPTER.mjs REPORT.json, then inspect the final painted result at full size and at normal speed. Retain a before/after crop around each reported defect and include other shots using the same rig.
