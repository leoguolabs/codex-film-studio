# Woodcut on laid paper

Begin on an inked block. Let a gouge bite and travel, its little curl of displaced wood just ahead of a line that widens under pressure. Pull back as the cuts spread. A raw block can carry backward lettering. Wet the block, lay a sheet against it, then lift the heavy sheet by an edge to read the impression in the right direction.

On a 1920 × 1080 sheet, keep the print between (36,36) and (1884,948). Set spoken lines in 44 px IM Fell English in the deep lower margin. Reserve English Small Caps for letters cut into the block. Aim for a low-key impression with broad passages of uninterrupted black. Draw narrow knife rims and tapering cuts with frayed edges. Arrange hill cuts downhill, curves around a body, and long horizontal sweeps across open air. Establish at least three depths in a place. Build a black silhouette around white skin with features left in ink. Keep shading attached to the moving surface. Sculpt the close view using a height field, then cut along its contours.

Choose one spot ink for the object driving the story. Apply it where the black block was cut away. Give it an entrance, a moment of greatest extent and a retreat. Let most of the film remain monochrome. The glassy black of the bare block and its honey-colored grooves should read differently from black ink on worn ivory stock. Keep paper and its outer edges fixed while moving over the block. Use short impression kicks at cuts. Animate a person's weight, reach and return on twos, while moving the camera and airborne marks at 24 fps.

Connect the wide view to a practical problem. Get close enough to see where the hand meets the material. Use held impressions for decisions, short tool inserts when the work accelerates and a final quiet, readable impression for the outcome. A low bowed note can carry across a cut. Save the sound characteristic of the subject until a visible action causes it. Score the voice sparingly, give wood, knife and paper distinct contacts and allow a moment of silence before the change.
