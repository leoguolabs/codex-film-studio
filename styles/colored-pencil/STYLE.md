# Colored-pencil science plate

Build a world the camera can actually enter. Start with a question answered by a visible physical change. Model the outside, the cut surface and the subject at a second scale. Establish a shared landmark. At a change of scale the graphite magnifier must surround the feature that will fill the following view. Keep the material coordinate system fixed within a shot. Move the camera in translation and animate actual objects on twos at 24 fps.

Retain paper highlights and a faint grain in every patch of pigment. Layer a broad side-of-lead field, short directional hatch marks, a cool crosshatch in shade, and sepia accents in deep shade. Use the engine's depth/normal break contour and rotating three-phase line jitter. Give broad forms curved tangents, stems longitudinal marks and strata diagonal marks. A distant form takes lighter pressure. Pull focus by easing the pressure on individual material IDs. Change scale through an expanding or closing graphite circle. Let layers arrive successively at a ragged drawing front, without dissolving the entire image.

Choose an environment with a discernible horizon, overlapping silhouettes, foreground detail, a hidden accessible interior and a legible physical route. Render the strata and a complete ecology or working environment around the hero. In an insert, remove geometry that would hide the process. Intentional scale exaggeration can be named in the narration. Distinguish the advance of an illustration from the rate at which a natural event happens. Check gravity, contacts, load paths and source/destination geometry at both scales. At a cutaway, give each visible face a single owner. Connect neighboring materials at shared vertices and match their edge subdivisions. Inspect adjacent frames during a camera move for patches that alternate between the two material colors. Track the same world points when measuring that change.

Keep at most one short handwritten label on a cream tag with a graphite leader. Set English narration on a quickly drawn cream slip in the lower left. Write a spare title into a clear patch of sky. Compose an intimate plucked score and quiet foley for drawing, the lens, the environment and decisive physical actions. Leave speech space. Inspect representative full-size frames, adjacent motion, text and the mix. For reference-matched commissions, decode each frame in both directions using matched shot roles, examine outliers, revise and repeat.

When a magnifier enters an object, replace the distant landscape with matter that could border the feature at the new scale. A nearby cut wall can take a warm pencil underlayer with lighter pressure than the subject. Keep the principal contact visible against it. Check a matched insert for large blank regions and inspect them at full size. At a spring, crevice or open channel, connect the cut face to its back wall and draw water as a surface with a bed. Show a soluble tracer through changing pigment in the water.

For a follow shot, identify the moving object and the background at a second depth. Start from a landmark the viewer already knows. Let the camera travel with the object while nearby forms move past and distant forms move more slowly. Keep the silhouette readable throughout a turn, release or landing. Review the adjacent frames at both ends and the middle of the move.

On the showcase page, give the music its own visible section. Identify the generator or performers actually heard, retain the brief and original recording, and show where the recording enters and leaves the film. Present the uncut recording and the picture-timed effects separately. Use field recordings appropriate to the location and small, timed Foley gestures for visible contacts. When matching another film’s sound, compare the narrator’s pace and pauses, the level of both music and ambience under speech, and the timing of visible contacts. Carry those relationships into the new setting with its own music and recordings. Check speech and pauses separately. Listen in the narration mix when a listening interface is available.
