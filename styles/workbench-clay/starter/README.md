# Expose a tabletop miniature

Requires macOS with Metal, Swift command-line tools, Python 3.11+, NumPy and FFmpeg. Build a film.py beside this README exporting DURATION in seconds and frame(stage, t). The renderer calls this function once per exposed pose. X is stage right, Y is up, +Z faces the lens. The palm of the default puppet is about one tenth of a stage unit across. The standing head reaches 1.86. Give the subject an original cast, props, action, spoken English and a timed sound mix.

Use stage.camera(eye, target, vertical_fov_degrees, focus=None, aperture=30). Focus is distance from camera. For a wide two-shot try a distance of 5 and a 28 degree lens, then adjust from the rendered image. A close-up works around 2.7 units and 22 degrees. Set stage.key to a world-space direction from subject to source; front-left is (-.63,.87,.59). stage.exposure controls the print density. Set stage.wood to RGB gain.

Import puppet, smooth, rot and the stage primitives from engine.clay. puppet(stage,position,hex_color, yaw=...,scale=...,head=(yaw,pitch,roll),lean=degrees,crouch=distance,gaze=(horizontal,vertical),blink=0..1,mouth=0..1,smile=0..1,hands=[left,right],feet=[left,right],nose=1,aspect=1,accessory=None) accepts performer-local targets. With a turn use rot(yaw).T @ (world_target-position)/scale to recover contact coordinates. Feet are sole centres. The accessory callback receives a stage at the head pivot. A relaxed pair of palms sits at x=±.36,y=.70,z=.075.

stage.egg(position,xyz_radii,color,angles=(yaw,pitch,roll)), box(position,xyz_half_extents,color,angles=...,bevel=.02), disc(position,radius,half_height,color,angles=...), ring(position,ring_radius,tube_radius,color,angles=...), rod(a,b,radius,color,end=tip_radius) work in their current coordinate frame. A disc or hoop's axis starts vertical. Every shape accepts mat (0 plain, 1 clay, 2 wood, 3 metal, 4 painted) and rough (0 glossy to 1 matte). A stage.transform(position,angles,scale) context composes local coordinates. In a stage.lump() context egg and rod shapes melt together with blend=.02 or more; a cut=True egg carves holes. Dress the stage with individually modelled production props. Optionally place an original background image in assets/workshop.jpg for a far-wall plate: the horizon is near the bottom and a textured wall occupies the upper 85%. The supplied shader also draws a brick wall without a plate.

Run python scripts/render.py --poses 0 35 80 --width 960 --output preview, inspect them and adjacent motion, then run python scripts/render.py. It writes numbered JPEG poses to poses/. Mix original dialogue, score and Foley to mix.wav with the same clock. Use python scripts/ffmpeg_serial.py -- -framerate 25/2 -i poses/%05d.jpg -i mix.wav -vf fps=25 -c:v libx264 -crf 17 -pix_fmt yuv420p -c:a aac -b:a 192k -movflags +faststart -shortest film.mp4. Use the guard for each FFmpeg launch. Retain the story sources, accepted voice WAVs and reproducible build inputs.

Use the pinned requirements.txt with Python 3.11+. The blank film has zero duration. Run python scripts/render.py --proof to inspect three neutral thumb-pressed samples. Place an optional original overhead timber photograph in assets/bench.jpg to project it in world coordinates. The copper, pigment and wear stay fixed while the camera moves. stage.collar(position, outer_radius, inner_radius, half_height, color, angles=...) creates a hollow band with separate inner and outer radii.

The puppet accepts blink and pucker in the range 0 to 1. At full closure the eyelid seam and corner creases replace the whites, and the lower lip follows the jaw. Drive short, posed changes from the performance and speech timing.

For a grip close-up, pass hand_shapes to puppet, keyed by -1 for the left hand or 1 for the right. Set amount from 0 to 1 and supply palm, wrist and elbow positions in puppet-local coordinates. Supply fingers as a list of (root, knuckle, pad) positions, thumb as (root, pad), and optionally yaw in degrees. Animate amount across the approach and release. Place the pads on the visible outside surface and deform a soft prop around that contact. Check the filmed view at peak compression and as the fingers leave.

### Dialogue and practical surfaces

`engine.visemes.SpeechMouths` accepts a list of takes, each with an `actor` and `phonemes` containing triples of IPA symbol, absolute start second and absolute end second. At each held pose call `at(actor, seconds)`. A returned mouth exposes `jaw`, `round`, `spread`, `seal` and `bite`; pass these to the puppet's `mouth`, `pucker`, `lip_spread`, `lip_seal` and `lip_bite` controls. Combine expressions with the take, and retain a neutral listening pose during silence. With the legacy Kokoro v1 int8 graph, an optional `scripts/expose_kokoro_timing.py SOURCE NEW_COPY` uses the Python `onnx` package. Use the timing-capable copy with the original voice and speed, and confirm reconstructed duration and short-time energy against the approved take before using its clock.

The optional mirrored wall uses `stage.wall_scale` in texture units per world unit. Choose a large enough backplate that the visible camera does not repeat a real prop. An optional `assets/prop-print.jpg` carries original package artwork. Material 8 maps a disc onto the top square of a 2:3 atlas. Material 9 wraps a side band onto the remaining lower third. Material 7 corrugates a thin foil collar and reflects the stage lighting. Other props can omit all three materials.

The actor accepts per-side `leg_shapes` to bend the knee and pitch the shoe. The foot target sets the sole height even when the toe rotates. Use `open_palm` to turn an existing hand target into a fan of soft finger pads. The woven surface uses material 10, with a paneled seam variant on material 11. Inspect the planted sole and the gap to nearby moving props in the final framing.

To add an original clay surface, develop an evenly lit, single-pigment swatch with `python scripts/develop_clay_relief.py SOURCE.png assets/clay-relief.png` using Pillow, NumPy and SciPy. Keep the original swatch and the recipe in the production. The renderer samples the estimated relief in each clay part’s own coordinate axes and filters it with distance. A large close-up and a small moving figure should both retain a quiet satin surface. The shader also supports a pinched triangular mouth and a left and right replacement lid of different thicknesses. Review the change from half-closed to fully closed, with the face turned toward its actual light.

### Eyelines and reverse angles

Pass a world-coordinate point in `puppet(..., look_at=point)` to turn the painted pupils towards a prop or another actor. Each eye uses its own position. `relaxed_lid` (0–1) lowers the lids while preserving the target. `neck_lift` raises the head and neck together in model units. Check the eye whites, pupil and eyelid edge from the actual camera throughout a turn. When a prop is held, anchor the thumb and individual pads to the prop’s rotating local axes.

For a perpendicular side wall, an optional production-owned `assets/end-walls.jpg` can supply two side panels. Assign material 13 or 14 to a thin upright box, rotate it into the wall plane and keep its local X/Y axes horizontal and vertical. The materials sample the left and right 55% of the image. Match its light direction to the back wall and inspect the corner in both reverse shots.

Wrap the puppet calls in `with stage.clay_bounce_card():` to add a broad, fixed front-right reflector to their clay surfaces. The renderer traces shadows towards the reflector, so the underside of a jaw, a hat brim or fingers over a prop can block its light. Expose the camera-facing side and the shadow side of the face before you set the final grade.

Set `pressed_face=True` with a `look_at` target to seat each white eye in a hand-pressed socket. The offset bead exposes a directional crescent. During closure the recess retreats behind the replacement lids. The figure has a two-part nose and a softly rounded mouth cut, with the edge radius reduced to zero on a full lip seal. Compare an open look, first closing exposure, full squeeze, bilabial and open vowel from the taking lens.

For portrait focus, pass a fresh list as `eye_marks=marks` to the speaking puppet. Position the camera, then call `stage.focus_on_nearest_eye(marks)`. Each mark carries the posed eye centre, orientation and radii. The lens uses the surface nearest the camera even when a crouch or lean carries the character forward.

Use `engine.contact.fixed_pivot_center(rest_center, rest_axes, turned_axes, local_pivot)` to turn a rigid part about a fixed support. Set the part’s orientation, recover its centre with this function and target the hand to the resulting pose. Check that the support stays fixed and the fingertips remain on the visible surface.

For a pressed portrait, use `pressure_lids=True` with `pressed_face=True`. The sculpted upper pad and lower lid gain depth during a strong simultaneous blink and pucker. The puckered muzzle has an integrated chin and a rounded cavity. To flag the fill on a portrait, use `with stage.clay_bounce_card(negative_fill=True):` for the entire camera setup. Check the shadow cheek at full blink and at full opening, and keep the exposure consistent across the shot.

Use the same animal rig for all shot sizes. Block the resting pose from observed anatomy and make the contact between the head, paws, torso and support visible from the taking lens. To diagnose an unexplained mark, render the same native-size frame with the body, covering and trim switched on separately. Check the assembled silhouette, eye state and contact again on both sides of the cut. When a sleeping animal draws a prop closer, keep the grip until the new support bears its weight.

For a replacement face, pass `mouth_corners=(left, right)` to `puppet()`. Values are signed vertical distances in puppet units. Start around 0.02 for a raised corner and −0.025 for a dropped one. Animate the corners with the intention of the line. The same rig accepts the existing `mouth`, `lip_seal`, `lip_spread` and `pucker` tracks. Use `scripts/render.py --samples-per-axis 2` to capture four optical samples before the lens and grain. The default setting captures one.

With `pressed_face=True`, `pressure_lids=True` and two authored `mouth_corners`, a strong pucker can bring the nose between the wider folded pads and raise the paired upper lip above the opening. A black card under the face can deepen the shadow beneath the nose. Wrap the puppet in `stage.clay_bounce_card(negative_fill="under-chin")` to enable this on a selected shot.

The pressure-lid casting has separate upper and lower rolls, a sloping nasal wall and tapering creases. Under-chin fill returns warm light to the cheek below the pad. For a grazing camera angle, inspect the far temple closely for a bright pinhole between the lid and cheek. The renderer gives these rays extra steps when the pressed lids close the head group.

Use `blink_on_twos(seconds, centre, hold=1)` on the 25 fps, two-frames-per-pose clock. The centre is in seconds and the hold counts complete exposed poses. Optional `approach` and `release` weights affect the immediately adjacent poses. The cast has three individually pressed strokes and samples the clay that shades their edges.

### Revising a finished cut

For fast actions or a mixed-cadence film, run `python scripts/render.py --pose-step 1`. It writes one image per output frame to `frames/`. Use `-framerate 25 -i frames/%05d.jpg` with the same serial encoder. In `film.frame`, evaluate fast action at the supplied time. For a held puppet pose, evaluate its rig at the previous even frame on the 25 fps clock while keeping contact and sound cues on their actual output frame. The default step of two still writes 12.5 poses per second to `poses/`. With either step, `--poses`, `--start` and `--end` count images in that sequence.

Map original time to output frame before editing the audio. Protect complete spoken takes at unit speed and mark the exact contacts. Use the map to re-expose fast poses through the taking camera and to place prop-recording onsets on the same clock. Render into a fresh directory before replacing an existing picture sequence. Verify every new frame, the relative level of speech and music, and the running time of the encoded audio and picture. Save a brief, real-speed A/B excerpt of the same action from the preceding and revised masters.

For a restrained cast revision, keep the approved puppet and performance. Set the clay colour on the same `puppet` call, then tune `aspect` or `nose` in small increments. Compare the same pose from the same camera before and after the adjustment. Inspect support and clothing contacts on the revised figure.
