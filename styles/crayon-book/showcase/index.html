02 / THE VISUAL LANGUAGE

Leave a mark.
Leave a little paper.

The feeling comes from how pigment meets paper, and how an action unfolds. Each layer belongs to the same physical world.

Aa
01 / MATERIAL

Paper with a memory.

Warm fibres, dry hatching and irregular gaps in the pigment. Grain and drawing move together with the camera; different forms keep their own stroke direction.

02 / DRAWING

Outline. Colour. Hold.

Trace the contour, then lay down colour in passes. Late props and lettering get their own drawing progress, followed by a hold long enough to read.

03 / MOTION

A page with a hinge.

Two paper surfaces, a gutter and contact shadows. Pull back, turn, settle and push in on one timeline, with the illustration anchored to its page.

04 / INSIDE THE STUDIO

Soft pictures.
Careful craft.

The paper, characters and sound need to belong to one continuous story.

01

Small actions, clear intent.

Let held poses, blinks, mouth shapes and necessary hand movements carry the performance. Give important actions time and preserve contact with props and the ground.

02

One shared clock.

Drawing, colouring, camera, speech and contact sounds share a frame timeline. Carry music across cuts, preserve note tails and place the page sound on the moment of contact.

03

Look. Watch. Listen.

Inspect the encoded grain, small lettering and continuous motion, then listen to the mix separately. Record what was actually checked, repair observed defects and verify the delivery file.
