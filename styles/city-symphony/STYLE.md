# City Symphony

Make a place legible through the time it takes things to move. Choose a repeating action and a position to return to as the light changes. Build a story from its particular machines, people, weather and history. Photograph it or license real location footage. A new subject needs a new selection, a new score and a new edit.

## Picture and time

At 30 fps, 120 BPM gives 15 frames per beat. Set the running time from the strongest available actions and the time the closing line takes to read. For a sample with no other duration request, three minutes is the upper limit. Finish as soon as the visual idea and closing line have landed. Honor any more specific running time in the brief. Give each shot a job in the progression. On the first assembly, cut duplicate angles of the same action, overlapping facts, long grid holds and neutral footage on either side of a brief gesture. Bring a view back when the light, historical period or action has changed. Fit the score to the surviving cut and protect natural speech and credit reading time. Let an establishing view hold for four to eight beats. Move from bars to beats, then half beats, then momentary subdivisions. Give a fragile motion the space of a sustained chord. Keep people in their environment. Preserve authentic place names, emblems and flags that belong to the shot. When a photographed brand needs a redraw, trace its visible shapes in the camera plate before the grade so the lettering, colors and position follow the moving sign.

Use real tripod time-lapse, on-board camera motion, street-height observation, close detail, an overhead, and an archival counterpart. Make the first view a high night view with a controlled falling push. Return to one fixed camera, including daylight and night when available. Blend neighboring exposures into streaks on faster motion. Keep poles and rails stable in slow motion through interpolated neighboring exposures. For a locked crowd or traffic view, isolate moving features, follow their centroids and briefly let fine amber paths take over the plate.

As the score fills, subdivide the screen from two plates to four, nine and sixteen. Keep a thin black gutter (about 4 px at 720p); distribute cell entrances across a fraction of a beat, showing the last composition under the arriving cells. Recut a few cells on sixteenths. Face yesterday and today across a growing central seam; put the archival year in the lower left. Save a brief two-frame day/night interchange and a reverse-order reprise in four- then two-frame flashes for the last ascent. Cut the picture to black on a musical accent. Carry the existing motif through the black and the printed document when they belong to one journey. For a specifically requested audible stop, let the preceding harmony decay. Reveal the closing line above moving footage. End on a darkened moving view with readable credits.

## Print, lettering and sound

Set 1.85:1 inside 16:9 with full-width black mattes. Bring the toe off black, ease the shoulder, mute saturation slightly and warm the highs. Preserve night sodium and cool ambient light. Use restrained bloom around bright areas, irregular midtone-weighted grain, gentle peripheral falloff and subpixel gate drift. Print dust and distressed borders would overpower modern footage. Use widely tracked warm-ivory Michroma for display and a light sans for small text, left inset near nine percent. Lay text over a soft local burn. On key counts, lock the digits or animate a short count-up, support the unit with a thin orange rule and cite the official record. Use a fine lower-right day ring, dim or suspend its hand at the brake, then rewind it during the flashback. Hold text long enough to read at actual viewing size. Keep the DCP-like friction of sharp type over a living photographic field.

Compose a distinct minimalist score on the picture's clock. Layer organ and low pedal, short woodwinds in triplets against eighths, bowed and short strings, a bell on digit locks, and horns in the final rise. Build harmony over bars, change voicings and the register between movements, and leave a quiet unresolved coda. An English voice requested for this form fits a single deliberately placed close-miked line in the coda. Keep the main montage score-led. Retain MIDI or an equivalent editable score, the mixed master and original take. Target approximately −14 integrated LUFS and keep decoded true peak at or below −1 dBTP.

For a score made in Gemini, open Create music through the requested browser and give the composer the cut's duration, grid entry, brake, number lock, visual blackout, intended musical continuity and spoken window. Keep the provider's complete playable file and prompt. Measure the decoded performance because its duration, silences and accents can differ from the timing described in chat. Trim musical phrases to the edit using source in-points and short crossfades. Place a real attack on the number lock. Let one continuous source passage cross the black, map and narration when musical continuity is wanted. Ease the music down before speech and recover over a phrase. If a repeat is needed, join matching beats and harmony with an overlap that carries the tail and avoids doubled transients. Check the transition in the final mix. Save the cue sheet, instrumental edit, dialogue stem and master in the film project. For a music-only replacement, compare the original and final picture and caption packets, then check loudness, peak and the spoken line in the delivered audio.

## Evidence

Keep all source pages, creators, terms, in-points and transformations beside the edit. Retain a verified primary source for each figure and quote. Use an actual scan or documented archival document in the coda. Inspect source cuts and title slates before selecting windows. Render a short moving test first. Review the final opening, recurring view, density build, grid, brake, archive, stop and coda as moving sections plus stills. Machine-scan every decoded frame and compare same-function sections in both directions when a reference is supplied. Keep picture, rhythm, voice and musical review distinct. Use this document and the blank kit alone for an independent film; preserve its initial MP4 and hash before opening comparative evidence.

For a longer story, place a distinct development before the break when the footage supports one. The blackout can be as short as a quarter to a third of a second. Let the document fill the aperture and reveal the relevant line or place within a second or two. Carry one moving background through the final credits. A long stretch of blank screen or a distant chart with empty margins changes the felt cadence. Lettering and a brief optical fall can share the opening night shot. For the two-frame interchange, choose a genuinely dark exposure and a bright exposure at a matching viewpoint. Step through the rendered alternation one frame at a time. Revisit one verified figure against a darker late view so its scale reads differently from a daylight count. On the moving coda, leave streetlights or skyline silhouettes visible as it enters from darkness, lift toward dawn, then lower the exposure under the credit blocks. Read the smallest credit line over the brightest part of that take.
