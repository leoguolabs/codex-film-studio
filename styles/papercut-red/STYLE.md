# Vermilion Paper Cut

Create an English cut-paper film in the visual language of red Chinese window flowers. Each frame should read as sheets that could be picked up by hand. The cuts through the red surface carry the inner drawing.

## Draw the material first

- Work at 24 fps. Move jointed bodies by changing the same rigid parts every two frames. Let parallax, camera, drifting debris and the backlight move every frame. Hold the face through an action and swap a separate expression piece.
- Build a shape as a closed cut contour and a second set of closed holes. Use short tapered slits, small crescent holes, punched petals, toothed seams and interrupted inner contours. Keep paper bridges at every junction and around eyes. Check neighbouring holes at macro scale for overlaps and stray islands outside the perimeter. Vary cut size so large holes read at wide scale and small cuts reward a close view.
- Offset the cut faces and layers by roughly 1–3 pixels at 1280×720. Put fine fibres and gently mottled pigment inside each part. Front-lit shapes stay opaque. Small contiguous areas of the world underneath should remain visible in the holes.
- Start with warm ivory against vermilion. For a night passage choose indigo paper for the ground and a few distinct depth plates, reserving red for the action. At wide scale use engraved details in three or more overlapping planes. Gold marks a single important accent. Match depth with dyed paper values.
- Keep the camera parallel to the surfaces. Scale and pan into the craft. For a fold or turn, shorten the active flap around its crease, shift its thickness and show its reverse. Keep both scissor handles visible through the open beat. Advance the cut at the tips, then let the offcut fall. Make the clearest cut or reveal cause something in the story.
- In a backlight passage let amber through actual holes while the sheet stays dark red. Attach the bright cut-outs to their exact apertures. A projected pattern can travel along the ground or another sheet. Keep the silhouette and apertures distinct across the brightest frames.
- Compose a high-contrast cut-letter serif title and one low red serrated paper strip for spoken captions. Use a warm bold serif. Time the caption from the retained voice and give it post-speech reading time. Curtain edges and foreground paper can carry transitions.

## Direct the scene and score

Choose a short original subject with one visible difficulty and one intelligible cut-paper solution. Design recognizable silhouettes for the subject, including any authorized figures, flags or brands needed for the story. Sketch wides, working hands, a profile action, an overhead craft view and a final clean result. Choose only the shots the story needs.

Write sparse English voiceover and leave space for the action. Use an intimate plucked-string and bamboo-flute-like score with an original pentatonic phrase and wood accents. Place scissors, a crease, flutter and paper contact at their actual frame cues. Let a short breath before a decisive reveal build anticipation. Let the scene carry its own sound after each line.

Review raw material proofs, encoded action, spoken cues and actual delivery. Pair equivalent visual roles when comparing different subjects. Inspect the release frames around each cut, limb contact, fold edge and held expression individually. Keep machine coverage of every frame and record precisely the visual and listening scope.
