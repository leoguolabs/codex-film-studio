# Marker on Paper

The camera looks straight down at warm uncoated white paper. Begin with the first real mark already moving into view. Let it describe a recognizable object before the question appears in the upper-left corner. Underline the title by hand, use a small handwritten note when it gives the viewer something new, and circle the page number high on the right. Allow stretches of almost empty paper.

Use a rounded, roughly eight-unit felt tip on a 1920 × 1080 sheet. Long strokes bow slightly. At the start and at a sharp turn, the ink pools. Different strokes crossing each other deepen in colour. A rapid row of fine diagonal hatches can ground an object or give an edge depth. Keep the medium dry and the page border outside the picture. The renderer supplies the paper fibres, nib, density and fixed individual-letter irregularities.

Give the black marker the contours, algebra and words. Introduce blue where a dimension is given. Mark the quantity being sought in red, resume that colour for the answer and let the final red line linger. Make the colour mean the same thing in the object, the calculation and the comparison. Draw an actual cross-section, mechanism or recognisable silhouette wherever it makes the number understandable. Keep most of the page open. Place a short regular-weight note beneath the heavier question when there is something else to say.

Record the English first. Tie each spoken idea to the mark that explains it. Headings are large bold Kalam (92–112 units), measurements and equations usually 48–76, small notes 34–42. Follow the stroke order of each glyph. Build arrows shaft first, then head. During holds the diagrams breathe on twos while lettering stays fixed. The camera moves on ones. Start around 1.5×, travel with the first outline, then pull level and wide for the question. Hold the whole comparison long enough to read.

Make a close inspection feel like entering a small feature in the first drawing. Charge into one compact detail over roughly three quarters of a second. Begin the next sheet with the same mark already there at the same apparent size, position, direction and ink width. Pull out to reveal its new context. Keep the important labels legible at settled camera positions. To change the question or compare cases, turn the paper from its bottom right corner in about 0.8 seconds. Let the back of the sheet catch the light and allow a brief silence. End with the answer circled or underlined, then pull back to the full working sheet and hold it.

Use English caption slips at the bottom of the glass. Split long sentences naturally over a larger first line and a smaller second line. Keep labels above the strip. Give the British English voice the foreground. A run of letters or hatches makes one soft, rounded felt-tip rub. Keep the paper turn on a separate stem and leave a short breath in the music under the fold. Choose an original instrumental with enough space for the narrator. Where Gemini music is requested, retain its original performance, prompt and edit points. Deliver the drawings, takes, stems, captions and encoded film together.

For a matched push, land the outgoing pose by the last encoded frame and hold the incoming pose across the boundary before pulling away. Compare the shared mark on both adjacent frames. At each settled zoom, check every line near all four edges as well as the main label. Carry the unknown into the middle diagram while the voice introduces it, then return to red in the calculation. Leave a nib-width gap between a circle around the result and the preceding equals sign.

Move in to read the actual measurement and calculation, pull out when the audience needs to compare, and go close again for the result. Check the settled framing after the camera reaches the edges of the paper. Small scraps of a neighbouring word can survive a mathematically centred move. Draw an underline from the measured beginning and end of its word. Leave a little paper between the letters and the oval that encloses the final answer. Add the small handwritten production credit during the last full-sheet hold.
