# Sketchnote

Draw one sheet of field notes from the inside out. Start close enough to read a single clue before the viewer knows what the sheet describes. Write the title on a folded ribbon. In the body, draw distinct objects with a few large contours and selective grey marker shadows. Write a number and a short action verb together on each yellow ribbon. A tab wide enough for both is readable from the poster view. Use broad arrows to connect the pictures. Leave enough white space around each drawing to read its silhouette with a moving camera.

Track one physical thing or quantity with the accent marker. The number tabs and arrows may share its colour. At the extreme close-up, a real surface should surround the accent: plan the metal, fibre or shell behind it as well as the little diagram inside. When the camera dives into a tiny part, the viewer should find information inside that was invisible at the panel scale. Use a match cut between two round or similarly shaped marks. Keep their centres and projected dimensions aligned on both sides of the cut. Try riding a moving outline when the next panel is introduced. For a comparison, let both states and their labels settle on screen together.

Plan backwards from the poster. At full view the ribbon, illustrated panels, opening clue and bottom conclusion should read in a few eye movements. Give the lower margin a quiet rule and a small handwritten credit. The close camera can legitimately crop a neighbour; keep each speaking subject and its own key label above the caption slip. In a large zoom change, travel with the subject so it stays in frame. Stop moving for the final reading hold and circle the opening clue.

The bundled renderer fixes the physical materials: warm dot paper, charcoal-coloured fineliner, three strengths of grey marker, and an accent. The dots stay fixed to the sheet. Fineliner strokes breathe on twos. Fixed translucent marker strokes expose their streaks on close views. Kalam Bold writes the keywords. Patrick Hand writes the small annotations and the English subtitle on a taped paper slip. The half-written letters follow actual glyph strokes. Draw the shadow, words and swashes in their proper layer order; register an underlining swash before its black label.

Record short English spoken lines early. A whole spoken sentence should fit on the bottom slip; break a long sentence at a natural breath. Space pen strokes and reading holds against the measured line clock. Begin with room for the dry pen sound; a sparse wooden mallet, plucked bass and bell arrangement can enter with the question. A glint at a reveal or a rolled chord at the pullback gives the music somewhere to go. Export the music and pen separately for review.
