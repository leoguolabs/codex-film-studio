# Ukiyo-e woodblock cinema
Build a series around one object whose appearance, placement or purpose changes. Let the viewer follow it from an open landscape into a working person's hands and back into the larger world. Hold a view long enough to read its silhouette. At an important turn, show what physically changes.

The sheet is 1920 × 1080. The narrow double sumi border is part of the print. Near objects can occupy half of it while tiny distant people remain legible. Organize by overlapping planes, oblique parallels, cropped foreground forms and a pale horizon. Draw foreground contours with taper and modest irregularity. Reserve strong detail for the human action or hero prop. Use the bundled procedural paper and block treatment on every large field, including large moving shapes.

Pick a family of indigo, muted blue, earth and green. A small amount of vermilion can identify a thread, tie or tool across several views. Leave the paper showing in highlights. Make one hand-wiped sky band with bokashi; use scattered fine linework, compressed silhouettes and patterned fabric or foliage to establish scale. Preserve the print's color even in night or rain. In a held shot a breeze can move the edge of a sleeve or ripple the surface without displacing the whole frame.

Play most character action at eight drawings a second, faster weather at twelve. The reading camera runs at 24. Slide through exposed mounting paper into the next sheet. Build a decisive gesture in stages and give the peak a readable hold. A hard silence on bare paper can give the next impression weight. The final pullback should reveal the actual sheets seen on the journey.

Keep English text on integrated cream slips. An upright plate number, a short English word down a narrow cartouche, one chipped red seal and a double ink rule work at print scale. Speak in concrete short clauses with generous space for the image and score. Use sparse buzzing plucks, breathing flute, wooden clacks and skin drums. Time cues to the scene's physical event. Keep music well under words and leave the still point truly quiet.

Inspect a wide, a prop insert, the decisive action, a transition through the mount, the blank and the end scroll. Check strokes and registration at delivery size. Frame-by-frame feature pairing can expose flat pictures, heavy outlines or camera judder. Record the concrete repair and repeat the comparison on the final export.

Keep the contour of an enlarged object crisp by drawing its paths at the scale of the closest view. If the story turns on a small physical detail, show the whole action first, hold its silhouette, then move towards that detail. Give the viewer time to see where the strain falls or where two surfaces meet.

At the held peak, check the whole frame again at thumbnail size. The thing that crosses the border should still read in silhouette from across the room. Anchor it to something the viewer already saw in the previous view. Keep the person or object receiving the impact visible until the camera moves closer.

Draw every illustration in code, including the cast, props, locations and textile motifs. Keep the facial contour, eyes, mouth and hair together in a rigid head drawing. Articulate arms and hands independently with fixed bone lengths. Check facial shape over a full gesture and in the encoded film after accounting for camera motion.
